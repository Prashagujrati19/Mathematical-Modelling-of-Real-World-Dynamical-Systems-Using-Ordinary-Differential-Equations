# Mathematical-Modelling-of-Real-World-Dynamical-Systems-Using-Ordinary-Differential-Equations
This project presents a comprehensive computational study of real-world dynamical systems using Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) and Wolfram Mathematica. It focuses on converting real-world phenomena into mathematical models, solving the resulting differential equations, visualizing their behavior over time, and interpreting the underlying dynamics.

A total of 18 mathematical models are developed across diverse areas, including population dynamics, technology adoption, environmental pollution, pharmacokinetics, epidemiology, warfare, and ecology. The models include exponential and logistic population growth, density-dependent growth, harvesting, lake and seasonal pollution, drug assimilation and repeated administration, influenza epidemic models, carrier-based disease transmission, battle models, and predator–prey systems.

For each case, relevant variables, parameters, assumptions, and initial conditions are identified and translated into first-order, nonlinear, or coupled differential equations. Wolfram Mathematica is used to solve these models computationally. The DSolve function is applied to obtain analytical solutions where possible, while NDSolve is used for nonlinear and coupled systems that require numerical methods.

The project generates numerical solution values and graphical representations to study system behavior over time. These visualizations help identify important dynamical characteristics such as growth, decay, saturation, equilibrium, convergence, oscillation, and interactions between populations. Classical models such as the logistic growth model, compartmental drug-assimilation model, SIR epidemic model, Lanchester-type battle model, and Lotka–Volterra predator–prey model are examined in detail.

Several models are further extended by introducing realistic factors such as harvesting, seasonal changes, external mortality, density dependence, persistent infection, disease carriers, and interactions among multiple species. Particular attention is given to how initial conditions and parameter values influence the resulting system behavior.

The project also highlights the importance of numerical diagnostics when solving computationally challenging models and emphasizes careful interpretation of simulation results and model limitations. Overall, the study demonstrates how mathematical theory, differential equations, computational simulation, numerical methods, and scientific visualization can be integrated to analyze complex real-world systems and gain practical insight into nonlinear dynamical behavior. The study strengthens understanding of how mathematical assumptions shape predictions, how computational tools support analytical reasoning, and how simulations can reveal patterns that may be difficult to observe in real-world systems. It also develops practical skills in modelling, coding, visualization, analysis, and scientific communication.
